Actor-Critic and Distributed RL: DDPG, TD3, SAC, and Beyond

Deterministic and soft actor-critic methods, distributed training

This part-level leaderboard zooms into the actor-critic and distributed RL families specifically; the broader cross-part RL leaderboard lives in rl/01 Related Work and is not duplicated here. Columns track design axes that distinguish methods within these two families rather than across the whole book: policy class (deterministic, stochastic Gaussian, max-entropy, V-trace-corrected on-policy), the number of critic networks (a load-bearing hyperparameter for overestimation-bias control1), and the two canonical scoreboards (MuJoCo HalfCheetah-v2 reward at 1M env steps for continuous control; Atari-57 median human-normalized score for distributed agents). Entries marked n.r. are not reported in the original paper because the method targets only the other domain; entries marked †\dagger come from third-party reproductions2 rather than the original paper.

The continuous-control column tracks a clean three-step monotone improvement driven by overestimation-bias control3. DDPG's single Q-network and OU exploration plateau near 3300; TD3 nearly triples this to 9636 by combining clipped twin critics, delayed actor updates, and target-policy smoothing; SAC pushes further to roughly 11000 by replacing the deterministic actor with a max-entropy stochastic policy whose temperature α\alpha is auto-tuned to match a target entropy. MPO arrives at a similar ballpark (∼\sim9000) via a different route: EM-style decoupled policy improvement with a non-parametric weighting step, which is empirically more robust to learning-rate choice but rarely beats SAC on raw HalfCheetah numbers. The critic-count axis is the single most predictive design choice on this benchmark: every method that crosses the 9000 line uses two critics with a min-target, and every method that stalls below 5000 uses one.

The distributed column is a different scoreboard with a different bottleneck. A3C ran 16 CPU workers asynchronously to reach 344% Atari median HNS in 4 days, and the A2C synchronous variant matched it on a single GPU once vectorized environments became cheap. ACER folded experience replay into A3C with truncated importance sampling and Retrace, but its Atari median was only marginally above A3C and it lost the dominant-on-policy-AC title to PPO/SAC within six months. IMPALA's V-trace correction broke the on-policy throughput ceiling: by sending behaviour-policy probabilities alongside actions and correcting at the learner, IMPALA scaled linearly to roughly 500 actors at 250k FPS, but its median 191.8% on Atari-57 trailed the prioritized-replay Ape-X / R2D2 line because off-policy correction is too aggressive at moderate staleness. R2D2 closed that gap to 1920% by adding LSTM Q-networks with the burn-in trick over Ape-X-style replay, accepting mild staleness as a regularizer rather than correcting for it. Agent57 then layered NGU intrinsic rewards plus a 32-arm meta-controller on top of R2D2, becoming the first agent superhuman on all 57 games at the cost of ∼\simUSD 10k compute per game. The actor-critic and distributed lines converge in Agent57: it is simultaneously a two-critic actor-critic (separate Qe,QiQ_e, Q_i heads) and a fully distributed agent (512 actors, single TPU learner), making it the natural terminal entry of this part.
