Trust-Region Policy Methods: TRPO, PPO, and GRPO

Trust-region and proximal policy optimization

The trust-region family is the longest-running active line in policy optimization, persisting from Schulman's 2015 KL-constrained natural gradient through the clipped surrogate that defines today's RLHF pipelines. Where the broader RL leaderboard in the policy-gradient foundations chapter surveys foundations, off-policy actor-critics, distributed and model-based methods together, this chapter narrows to one design axis: how each variant enforces or approximates a trust region on the policy update. TRPO enforces DKL≤δD_{KL}\!\le\!\delta exactly via line-search; PPO-KL adds −βDKL-\beta D_{KL} as a soft penalty with β\beta adapted by target-KL feedback; PPO-clip drops the KL apparatus and clips the ratio rt∈[1−ϵ,1+ϵ]r_t \in [1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon] (an empirically softer trust region whose measured KL routinely exceeds the bound ϵ\epsilon implies); ACKTR substitutes K-FAC for conjugate gradient; GRPO removes the critic entirely. The methods below differ on four dimensions covered in detail across the chapter: (1) how the KL bound is imposed (hard line-search, soft penalty, clipped ratio, or none), (2) the surrogate objective form, (3) whether a learned critic is required, and (4) the domain (MuJoCo locomotion, Atari, MOBA games, or LLM reasoning) where the variant was first deployed.

Reading the table chronologically traces the trust-region trade-off curve. TRPO 2015 sets the operational ceiling on KL enforcement: a hard δ=0.01\delta = 0.01 bound enforced by conjugate gradient on the Fisher-vector product plus a backtracking line search, giving the monotonic-improvement guarantee at the cost of second-order machinery and ~10 CG iterations per update. ACKTR keeps the natural-gradient direction but replaces CG with K-FAC's block-diagonal Kronecker factorization, getting roughly 4900 HalfCheetah reward at comparable wall-clock to PPO. PPO-clip 2017 then drops the KL apparatus entirely in favor of a clipped ratio surrogate, and the empirical reality (already noted in (Engstrom et al. 2020)) is that measured KL routinely exceeds what ϵ\epsilon would imply; what actually limits divergence is the small epoch count plus learning-rate annealing, with the clip acting as a soft attenuator on outlier gradients. PPO-KL is the original paper's adaptive-penalty variant; it underperforms the clip on standard MuJoCo and is rarely used outside the DPPO terrain-locomotion regime where the asymmetric β\beta feedback was load-bearing.

The 2020+ rows extend the family to two new domains where vanilla clipping breaks. Dual-clip PPO addresses an off-policy-PPO failure mode at large importance ratios by adding a lower bound cÂtc\hat{A}_t when Ât<0\hat{A}_t < 0, reducing divergence from roughly 15% to 3% of seeds in the JueWu Honor of Kings stack without changing final policy quality. GRPO eliminates the critic entirely by sampling G=64G\!=\!64 outputs per prompt and using within-group reward normalization as the advantage estimate; this halves training memory for LLMs (where critic-of-policy-size is prohibitive) and uses an unbiased KL estimator πref/πθ−log⁡(πref/πθ)−1\pi_{ref}/\pi_\theta - \log(\pi_{ref}/\pi_\theta) - 1 rather than the log-ratio form, becoming the de facto RLHF backbone for reasoning models. The two-decade arc shows convergence on a single recipe: clipped or KL-penalized importance-weighted surrogates with code-level optimizations (Engstrom et al. 2020) doing more empirical work than the algorithmic differences, and the chapter on implementation details covers which optimizations dominate.
